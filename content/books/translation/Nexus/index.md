## About the book

Yuval Noah Harari traces the systems that have allowed large societies to gather information, coordinate action, and exercise power. His account ranges from oral traditions and sacred texts to administrative records, mass propaganda, computer networks, and the rise of artificial intelligence.

At the center of the book is a warning: more information does not automatically produce more truth or wisdom. By comparing democratic and totalitarian networks and examining the new agency of algorithms, Harari asks what institutions are needed to correct errors, preserve human judgment, and keep powerful information systems accountable. This edition is translated into Persian by IO.
