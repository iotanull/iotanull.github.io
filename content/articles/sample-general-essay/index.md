The most productive shifts in science often begin quietly: not with a new answer, but with a better way to state the question. A change of coordinates, a new invariant, or a more careful distinction between observation and explanation can make an old difficulty look newly approachable.

## The shape of an explanation

A physical model does more than summarize measurements. It identifies which differences matter and which can be treated as changes of description. In mechanics, the same motion can look different from two frames while preserving a common structure.

{{< pullquote >}}A good representation does not merely shorten a calculation; it reveals what the calculation is about.{{< /pullquote >}}

Consider the compact relation

$$E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4.$$

Its value is not decorative symbolism. The equation gathers several regimes into a single statement and makes their relations visible.

## From formalism to understanding

The gap between a successful formula and an explanation is a philosophical as well as a scientific question. We can ask what the model represents, which idealizations it depends on, and how its claims connect to possible observations.
